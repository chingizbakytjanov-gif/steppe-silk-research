1. Summary

Trade between Kazakhstan and China more than doubled, from $15.4 billion to $34.1 billion. Kazakhstan still sells mostly raw materials and buys machinery, electronics and vehicles. Since 2023 it has run a trade deficit with China, which reached $3.7 billion in 2025.

2. Main results

  1. Trade turnover grew from $15.4 billion in 2020 to $34.1 billion in 2025, by 121%.
  2. China's share of Kazakhstan's foreign trade rose from 18.1% to 23.7%.
  3. Kazakhstan exports mainly ores, oil, copper and uranium, and imports machinery, electronics and vehicles.
  4. The balance moved from a surplus of $2.6 billion in 2020 to a deficit of $3.7 billion in 2025.

Imports from China nearly tripled, from $6.4 billion to $18.9 billion. Exports rose by about two thirds, from $9.0 billion to $15.2 billion. The balance changed sign in 2023. In 2025 imports grew by 24% and exports by 2%.

4. Structure of trade

All five main exports are raw materials or products of primary processing, and their value depends on world commodity prices. All five main imports are finished or semi-finished goods.

5. Kazakh and Chinese statistics

The two countries record the same trade differently. In 2025 China reports $48.7 billion of trade with Kazakhstan, and Kazakhstan reports $34.1 billion. Most of the difference is in goods leaving China: $29.7 billion in Chinese data against $18.9 billion arriving in Kazakh data.

6. Author's comment

I think it is not good that Kazakhstan sells China mainly raw materials, because this makes our economy heavily dependent on resources that will not last forever. I also see the trade deficit as a concern, because Kazakhstan now relies on China for machinery, equipment and technology. From what I have seen, much of the equipment in Kazakh factories and hospitals is Chinese. At the same time, these imports bring technology that Kazakhstan needs, so the answer is not to trade less with China, but to develop the relationship further and learn to produce more of these goods at home.

Methodology

  • Kazakh series: Bureau of National Statistics, Ministry of Trade and Integration and State Revenue Committee, as published by government bodies and national media. Figures for 2020 and 2021 come from news reports citing these bodies and are less reliable than later years. Turnover for 2022 is the sum of exports and imports. The 2023 import figure ($16.8 billion) is an early estimate, later revised to about $15.4 billion.
  • Chinese series: General Administration of Customs of China (GACC), monthly bulletin table "Imports and Exports by Country (Region) of Origin/Destination", December issue of each year, converted from US$ thousand to US$ billion.
  • Product groups: Kazakhstan's figures reported to UN Comtrade, 2024.
  • The two national series are shown side by side and never combined. Changes and shares are calculated by the author.
