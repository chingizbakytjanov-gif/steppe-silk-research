Raw Materials Out, Machines In: Kazakhstan's Trade with China, 2020-2025

Trade more than doubled in five years, and a surplus turned into a $3.7 billion deficit.

Summary

Trade between Kazakhstan and China more than doubled, from $15.4 billion to $34.1 billion. Kazakhstan still sells mostly raw materials and buys machinery, electronics and vehicles. Since 2023 it has run a trade deficit with China, which reached $3.7 billion in 2025.

Key findings

  1. Trade turnover grew from $15.4 billion in 2020 to $34.1 billion in 2025.
  2. China's share of Kazakhstan's foreign trade rose from 18.1% to 23.7%.
  3. Kazakhstan exports mainly ores, oil, copper and uranium, and imports machinery, electronics and vehicles.
  4. A surplus of $2.6 billion in 2020 turned into a deficit of $3.7 billion in 2025.

Imports from China nearly tripled, from $6.4 billion to $18.9 billion, while exports rose by about two thirds. The balance changed sign in 2023, and in 2025 the deficit reached $3.7 billion as imports grew by 24% and exports by only 2%.

All five main exports are raw materials or products of primary processing, so their value depends on world commodity prices. The main imports are finished goods.

A note on the statistics

The two countries record the same trade very differently. China reports $48.7 billion of trade with Kazakhstan in 2025, against $34.1 billion in Kazakhstan's own data. Most of the gap is on the Chinese export side.

My view

I think it is not good that Kazakhstan sells China mainly raw materials, because this makes our economy heavily dependent on resources that will not last forever. I also see the trade deficit as a concern, because Kazakhstan now relies on China for machinery, equipment and technology. From what I have seen, much of the equipment in Kazakh factories and hospitals is Chinese. At the same time, these imports bring technology that Kazakhstan needs, so the answer is not to trade less with China, but to develop the relationship further and learn to produce more of these goods at home.

Notes and method

  • The main series are Kazakhstan's official statistics (Bureau of National Statistics, Ministry of Trade and Integration, State Revenue Committee), as published by government bodies and national media.
  • Figures for 2020 and 2021 come from news reports citing official bodies and are less reliable than later years. Turnover for 2022 is the sum of exports and imports.
  • The 2023 import figure ($16.8 billion) is an early estimate that was later revised to about $15.4 billion.
  • Product groups are Kazakhstan's figures reported to UN Comtrade. Chinese customs data are shown separately and never mixed with the Kazakh series.
